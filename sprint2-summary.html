1 · The task

Read the subscription, write the bill

Input and output

Each customer has 743 subscription options (CRM columns, 0/1) and a bill of 731 items (BIL columns, 0/1). The model reads the options and fills the 731 boxes. This is multi-label classification.

The grade: EMR

A bill counts only if all 731 boxes are right. 730 right and 1 wrong = wrong bill.

"Boxes right" always looks high (97–99%) because most boxes are 0. Only EMR decides.
2 · What we found in the data

Five facts that shaped our decisions

Wrong bills

Some customers with the same subscription have different bills: the provisioning errors the professor mentioned. We fix them with a vote.

Broken profiles

4,728 train customers have no subscriber type/status, or two. This never happens in test or in the solution, so they are errors.

Many copies

Most rows are exact copies of others (popular plans). Removed only after the vote, so every customer could vote.

Rare items

Most billing items are active for less than 1% of customers. That's why "boxes right" always looks high and why EMR is hard.

Test is new

Only 5 of 97,100 test subscriptions appear in train, and only 3.5% of test bills. The model must build bills from rules, not copy them.

2 useless columns

CRM_2396_PACK and CRM_2398_PACK are always 0 in train. Removed from train and test.

Why B wins

Each item gets its own simple rule ("if the customer has option X, bill item Y"). That works for subscriptions the model has never seen.

Why the others fail

The Copier copies the neighbor's small differences. The Rule Tree mixes all 731 items in one set of rules and can't specialize. A multi-output Random Forest ran out of memory.
